Editorial standards

Last updated: 14 November 2026

This page explains how AniPix Blog is produced: who writes it, where the information comes from, how it is checked, how mistakes are corrected, and how the images are sourced. If you want to know whether something here is worth trusting, this is the page to read first.

Who writes this site

AniPix Blog is written and published by Lavkush Kumar, who also builds and maintains the AniPix wallpaper app. It is a one-person publication. There is no newsroom, no freelance pool and no sponsored content programme.

The practical consequence is that the range of subjects is narrow on purpose. Everything published here is about anime, gaming, and the specific problem of making artwork look right on a phone screen. We do not cover topics we have no first-hand experience with.

How articles are researched

Every article falls into one of two groups, and they are researched differently.

  • Practical guides (cropping, resolution, live wallpapers, choosing an app) are written from direct use. The settings, sizes and steps described are ones that have been tried on real devices. When a method only works on some phones, the article says which.
  • Anime and game write-ups are built from primary sources where possible: official sites, studio and publisher announcements, broadcast schedules, and the work itself. Where a claim depends on a third-party figure — a ranking, a vote share, an audience count — the source and the date are named in the article, because those numbers change weekly.

Where we are unsure, we say so in the text rather than rounding the uncertainty away. Where a release date or episode count has not been confirmed by an official source, the article marks it as unconfirmed instead of presenting it as fact.

How AI tools are used

We use AI tools in the production process, and we would rather state that plainly than leave readers guessing.

In practice this means AI assistance with drafting, structuring and language editing. It does not mean articles are published unread. Every piece is reviewed before it goes live: facts are checked against the sources above, claims that cannot be supported are cut, and anything that reads as generic filler is rewritten or removed. A published article is one a human has read and stands behind.

We do not publish AI-generated images presented as real photographs, and we do not invent quotes, sources, review scores or sales figures. If a number appears in an article, it came from somewhere we can point to.

Images

AniPix Blog uses two kinds of image, and they are treated differently.

  • Original graphics. Charts, diagrams and comparison tables are made for this site. Where an article includes a chart, the data behind it is named in the caption or the surrounding text.
  • Artwork and photography. Anime and game artwork is the property of its respective rights holders and is used here for commentary, identification and instructional purposes — showing what a wallpaper looks like, how a crop behaves, or what a character design consists of. Space and science imagery is credited to its source (NASA, ESA, ISRO or Wikimedia Commons) and is public domain unless stated otherwise in the caption.

If you hold rights to an image on this site and want it removed or credited differently, write to us using the contact page and it will be handled.

Corrections

Articles are updated when the underlying facts change — a new episode airs, a release date moves, a ranking shifts. Pages that are actively maintained carry a "last updated" date, and the date is changed only when the content actually changes.

If you spot an error, tell us through the contact page with the article name and what is wrong. Corrections that affect a factual claim are fixed in the article itself, not appended as a note at the bottom.

Advertising and independence

This site is supported by advertising. Adverts are served by third parties and are clearly separated from editorial content. We do not publish paid reviews, we do not accept payment for a positive write-up, and no advertiser has any say in what an article says. Where an article mentions the AniPix app, that is disclosed in the text, because the person writing it also builds it.

Contact

Questions, corrections and rights requests all go to the same place: the contact page. We read everything and reply to most things.
