As a Reincarnated Aristocrat - Season 3 Review (Airing Now)

Anime11 October 202611 min read
Share

Last updated: 11 October 2026 - Season 3 is currently airing

Season 3 of As a Reincarnated Aristocrat, I'll Use My Appraisal Skill to Rise in the World began on 27 September 2026, and it arrives with the biggest production change in the series so far. This is a live review: we are updating it as each episode airs, so bookmark it and come back weekly.

Quick facts

  • Season 3: began 27 September 2026, airing weekly on the Agaru Anime block (CBC / TBS)
  • Studio: GeyeG Pictures - taking over from Studio Mother, with the same core staff retained
  • Opening: "Still Water" by Rico Sasaki  |  Ending: "Hibi o Koete" by Akuruyo no Hitsuji
  • Broadcast: Sundays, late-night slot (23:30 JST)
  • Streaming: Crunchyroll  |  Genre: Isekai, political fantasy, war

Production note: On 4 September 2026 it was announced that GeyeG Pictures would take over animation production from Studio Mother for Season 3, while the same staff stayed in place. On screen, the transition has been smoother than most studio changes - the character designs and colour work are consistent with the first two seasons.

Ratings so far

  • AniPix score: 8.1 / 10 (early - provisional)
  • AniList: around 71-73% average
  • MyAnimeList: hovering around 7.4, in line with Season 2

Two episodes in, the score is roughly where Season 2 landed. That is a good sign: the audience is not treating the studio change as a break in quality. We will revise this as the season develops.

Where the story stands

Season 2 ended with the war over Verzud and a reshaped political landscape. Season 3 opens with Ars having been formally recognised for his service in the Missian succession war and appointed headman of Canarre - a promotion that sounds like a reward and functions like a target painted on his back.

He is no longer fighting for a faction. He is now responsible for an entire district: its people, its defences and its future. And the season's central threat arrives almost immediately - Seitz province, allied with Basamark, marches a huge army towards Canarre.

Episode by episode

Episode 25 (S3 Ep 1) - Headman

Aired 27/28 September 2026

Ars settles into his role as Canarre's headman and takes stock of what he has inherited. He brings in new allies and begins pushing a plan to make the district genuinely better rather than just better defended - then names a surprising choice as the new lord of Ramberk.

Take: A confident, calm opener. It is almost entirely setup, but it does the job Season 1's opening struggled with: it establishes the new status quo in a single episode without feeling like homework.

Episode 26 (S3 Ep 2) - Canarre in Crisis

Aired 4 October 2026

The new governor of Seitz marches on Canarre, aiming to cut the district away from Couran's forces. The scale of the approaching army becomes clear, and Ars and his people prepare for a fight they cannot avoid.

Take: The threat lands properly. This is the first time Canarre itself - not a distant fortress - is the thing in danger, and it changes the emotional weight of every scene.

What is working

The promotion actually means something. Making Ars a headman rather than a soldier is the smartest structural choice the series has made. It puts him in charge of logistics, people and politics at the same time, which is exactly where his skill set is strongest and most limited.

The studio change is invisible. GeyeG Pictures has matched Studio Mother's look closely enough that the season feels continuous. Character designs, palette and the general visual grammar are all intact.

The new opening is strong. "Still Water" by Rico Sasaki is a better fit for the show's more mature third-season tone than either previous OP, and the ending theme complements it well.

Seitz is a good antagonist. A province that allies with Basamark and moves on Canarre is a threat with a reason behind it, not just a bigger army.

What to watch out for

It is early. Two episodes is not enough to judge a season, and the opening of Season 3 is deliberately calm. If Season 2 taught us anything, it is that this show starts slow and pays off later.

Cast management. Ars now has a large retinue and a district full of new faces. Keeping all of them meaningful across a full cour is the hardest thing the writers have attempted so far.

Provisional verdict

Season 3 has started exactly the way a third season should: by making the protagonist's situation genuinely new. Ars is not chasing talent or fighting a war for someone else any more - he is responsible for a place, and an army is coming for it. If the season keeps this focus, it has a real chance of being the best of the three.
