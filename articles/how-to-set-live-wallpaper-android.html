How to Set a Live Wallpaper on Android (Step by Step)

Guides19 August 20265 min read
Share

This guide walks you through setting a live wallpaper on any modern Android phone, including the differences between Samsung, Xiaomi, Pixel and OnePlus software. It also covers how live wallpapers affect battery life and what to do if your phone does not seem to offer the option at all. Every step below uses the actual menu names you will see on screen.

What a live wallpaper actually is

A live wallpaper is not a video file you set like a photo. On Android it is a small application that draws to the home screen continuously, using the system's wallpaper service. That is why live wallpapers appear in a different place from ordinary photos: they are installed and selected as apps, not chosen from your gallery.

Because it runs as a service, a live wallpaper keeps using the processor and sometimes the graphics processor while your home screen is visible. A subtle animated gradient costs almost nothing. A full particle simulation with dozens of moving characters costs real battery. Knowing this difference explains most of the advice further down.

The standard way to set one

The core path is the same on almost every phone, though the wording shifts slightly.

  1. Open the home screen and press and hold on an empty area until the wallpaper menu appears.
  2. Tap Wallpapers (some phones say Wallpaper & style or Wallpapers & themes).
  3. Choose the Live or Live wallpapers category rather than Gallery or My files.
  4. Select a wallpaper to preview it. Many let you tap Settings or a gear icon to change colour, speed or motion.
  5. Tap Set wallpaper, then choose Home screen, Lock screen or Home and lock screen.

If you are installing a third-party live wallpaper from a store, the flow is usually: install it, open it once, then return to the wallpaper picker and it will appear in the Live list. Some apps also provide a Set wallpaper button directly inside the app, which skips the picker entirely.

Differences between manufacturer versions

Android is customised heavily by each brand, so the exact words differ.

  • Samsung (One UI): Press and hold the home screen, tap Wallpaper and style, then Change wallpapers. Live wallpapers are grouped under a Live wallpapers heading. Samsung also offers Wallpaper services that animate the lock screen.
  • Xiaomi (MIUI and HyperOS): Long-press the home screen, tap Wallpapers, and switch to the Live wallpapers tab. Xiaomi's own dynamic wallpapers live here, along with any you have installed.
  • Google Pixel: Long-press the home screen and tap Wallpaper & style. Tap Change wallpaper and pick the Live category.
  • OnePlus and Oppo: Long-press the home screen, tap Wallpapers, then Live wallpaper. ColourOS and OxygenOS place them in the same list as static options but label them clearly.

On any of these, if you only see static images, the Live category may be hidden behind a more arrow or a horizontal tab you have to swipe.

Battery and performance advice

A live wallpaper is always running when the home screen is visible, so it competes with everything else for power. A few practical rules keep the cost low.

  • Prefer wallpapers that pause when you open an app. Well-built ones stop drawing when they are covered.
  • Avoid full-screen video loops. A looping video at sixty frames per second is the heaviest common option.
  • Turn down or disable parallax and motion effects in the wallpaper's own settings if your phone feels warm.
  • Use the lock screen only. A lock-screen-only live wallpaper draws for a few seconds at a time instead of whenever the home screen is up.
  • On phones with an always-on display, do not combine it with a bright animated live wallpaper; the two together drain noticeably overnight.

When your phone does not support live wallpapers

Some budget phones and a few heavily modified versions of Android ship without the live wallpaper picker. If the Live category is missing entirely, try these in order.

  1. Install a live wallpaper app and open it directly. Many provide their own Set as wallpaper button, which does not need the system picker.
  2. Check Settings, then Apps, then the three-dot menu, and look for Default apps or Home app settings; on some phones the launcher, not the system, controls the picker.
  3. Install a third-party launcher such as Nova or Lawnchair, which restores the standard live wallpaper chooser on most devices.
  4. If none of that works, the device genuinely lacks the wallpaper service. In that case use a high-quality static wallpaper instead. A still image at the correct resolution will always look sharper than a poorly supported animation.

Getting the best quality

Resolution matters more than most people expect. A live wallpaper that is stretched from a small source will look soft on a modern 1080p or 1440p panel. Check that the source art is at least as tall as your screen in pixels, and prefer portrait-oriented sources for phones. If a wallpaper app offers a choice of quality, pick the highest and let the phone downscale rather than upscale.

Frequently asked questions

Do live wallpapers drain battery quickly?

It depends entirely on what they draw. A simple animated gradient might cost a few percent over a full day, while a video loop with particles can cost noticeably more. If you notice your phone running warm or losing charge faster, switch the wallpaper to the lock screen only or choose a lighter one.

Can I use a video as a live wallpaper?

Not directly. Android does not accept a raw video file as a live wallpaper. You need an app that plays the video as a live wallpaper service, or a launcher feature that supports video backgrounds. These are heavier than drawn animations.

Why is the live wallpaper option missing on my phone?

Either the manufacturer removed the picker, or your current launcher has replaced it. Try setting the wallpaper from inside a dedicated app, or install a third-party launcher that restores the standard chooser. If the wallpaper service itself is absent, the device cannot run live wallpapers.

Will a live wallpaper slow down my phone?

On a mid-range or flagship phone from the last few years, a normal live wallpaper will not make the interface feel slow. On very old or very low-memory devices, the extra drawing can cause stutter when scrolling the home screen, in which case a static wallpaper is the safer choice.
