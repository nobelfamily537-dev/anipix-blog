Naruto Shippuden Part 3: Season 3 (Episodes 54-71) - Every Episode Explained

Anime20 October 202612 min read

Last updated: 20 October 2026

This is Part 3 of the complete Naruto Shippuden episode-by-episode guide, covering Season 3: Episodes 54 to 71 - the Twelve Guardian Ninja arc. This is the first anime-only season of Shippuden, and the one where Naruto actually learns to use wind release.

Every episode below has a short summary and a note on how it holds up. For the arc-level overview, see the complete arc guide.

Season 3 at a glance

  • Episodes: 54-71
  • Arc: Twelve Guardian Ninja
  • Type: Anime-only (filler), but it contains the wind-training setup that matters later
  • Aired: 3 April - 14 August 2008
  • Opening theme: "Blue Bird" by Ikimono-gakari
  • Season score: 6.5/10

Let us be honest about this one: Season 3 is filler. It is not in the manga, it does not advance the Akatsuki plot, and if you skip it you will still understand everything that follows. But it is not pointless filler either. The wind-release training that runs through it is what eventually makes the Rasen Shuriken possible, and the arc introduces Sora - a character built to mirror Naruto - which gives the season a real emotional thread.

Every episode explained

Episode 54: Nightmare

Naruto wakes from a nightmare about his own Version 2 form tearing through everything around him. Sai, meanwhile, is in the village library reading up on how friendships actually work - and promptly calls Sakura "ugly". Kakashi tells Naruto they are going to build a brand new jutsu, stronger than the Rasengan, that only Naruto can use.

Note: A reset episode after the Tenchi Bridge. The wind-training promise it plants pays off later in the season.

Episode 55: Wind

Training begins. Kakashi explains nature transformation - how a ninja learns which element their chakra leans towards, and how to add it to an existing technique. Naruto finds out his affinity is wind, which sets the whole season's goal: a wind-style Rasengan.

Note: The most important setup episode of the arc, and a genuinely good explanation of how chakra works.

Episode 56: Squirm

Naruto keeps grinding at his wind training while a separate thread opens: graves are being robbed outside the village, and the culprits are looking for something specific. Asuma's own history starts to surface.

Note: The season shifts from training to plot. It takes its time getting there.

Episode 57: Deprived of Eternal Sleep

The grave robbers' target becomes clearer - the tombs of the Twelve Guardian Ninja, the twelve warriors who once protected the Feudal Lord. Konoha's team is sent to the Fire Temple, where they meet a young monk named Sora.

Note: Sora's introduction is the real start of the arc.

Episode 58: Loneliness

Sora is angry, prickly and clearly out of place - and Naruto recognises the feeling immediately, because he grew up the same way. The two of them rub each other the wrong way.

Note: The Naruto-Sora parallel is the emotional core of the season, and it lands.

Episode 59: A New Enemy

A group calling itself Team Furido steps forward as the force behind the grave robbing. They are not after money - they have a political target in mind.

Note: Finally gives the arc a face to fight.

Episode 60: Impermanence

The Fire Temple is attacked and the tombs are raided. Sora is caught in the middle of it, and the reason he is being targeted becomes clearer.

Note: The arc's first proper action beat.

Episode 61: Contact

Naruto and Sora clash directly, and Sora's rage proves to be far more dangerous than anyone expected. There is something unusual about his chakra.

Note: Sets up the season's real mystery.

Episode 62: Teammate

Forced to work together, Naruto and Sora slowly start to function as a unit rather than as two people shouting at each other.

Note: A quieter episode, but the character work is solid.

Episode 63: The Two Kings

The past of the Twelve Guardian Ninja is laid out, and with it Asuma's connection to them. The arc's central idea appears: the pull between the Feudal Lord's authority and the Hokage's.

Note: The most interesting thematic episode of the season.

Episode 64: Jet Black Signal Fire

Team Furido's plan moves into its next phase, and Konoha itself becomes the target rather than the temple.

Note: Raises the stakes from a local dispute to a village-level threat.

Episode 65: Lockdown of Darkness

The village goes into lockdown as the enemy moves inside it. The team is split up and forced to react.

Note: A tense middle episode that keeps the arc moving.

Episode 66: Revived Souls

The enemy's technique brings back fighters who should not be on the battlefield at all, and Konoha's ninja find themselves facing people they know.

Note: A genuinely unsettling turn for a filler arc.

Episode 67: Everyone's Struggle to the Death

The fights spread out across the village, with each member of the team taking on their own opponent. Nobody gets an easy win.

Note: One of the better action episodes of the season.

Episode 68: Moment of Awakening

Naruto's wind training finally starts to click, and Sora's power surfaces in full. The two threads of the season meet.

Note: The payoff episode for everything the season has been building.

Episode 69: Despair

The situation turns badly against Konoha, and the team is pushed to the edge of what they can handle.

Note: Bleak, and effective for it.

Episode 70: Resonance

Sora's chakra resonates with something inside Naruto - the same thing that has defined Naruto's whole life. It forces both of them to confront what they actually are.

Note: The emotional peak of the arc.

Episode 71: My Friend

The arc closes. Sora makes his choice and leaves, and Naruto's wind training reaches the point where the new jutsu becomes possible. The season ends on Naruto calling Sora a friend.

Note: A warm ending that earns its place, even in a filler season.

Season 3 verdict

This is the season people argue about. On one hand, it is a detour: 18 episodes that the manga never needed, and the middle stretch moves slowly. On the other hand, the wind training is genuinely important, Sora is a better character than most filler gets, and the theme - what makes a king, and what a village owes the people it protects - is more thoughtful than the arc gets credit for.

If you are watching Shippuden for the first time and want the essential run, you can skip most of this season and read a summary. If you are watching for the characters, it is worth the time.

Season 3 score: 6.5/10. Skippable, but not bad.
